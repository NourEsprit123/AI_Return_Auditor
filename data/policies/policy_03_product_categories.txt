# TEST POLICY — PRODUCT CATEGORY-SPECIFIC GUIDANCE

**Policy ID:** TEST-CAT-003  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** APPROVED FOR TESTING ONLY  
**Effective Date:** 2026-01-01

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This document provides supplementary assessment guidance for different product categories.

The general return and damage policies remain applicable to every category.

The guidance below helps identify relevant evidence. It does not create an automatic right to a refund or override another approved policy.

## 2. Electronics and Digital Devices

Examples include smartphones, laptops, tablets, monitors, cameras, and accessories.

Relevant visible issues may include:

- Cracked screens or housings
- Broken connectors
- Dents or scratches
- Missing visible components
- Damaged packaging

Useful evidence may include a clear photograph of the damaged area and a wider photograph identifying the device.

A photograph alone cannot establish whether an electronic device functions correctly.

If a claim concerns a hidden internal defect or a functional failure that cannot be verified visually, request appropriate additional evidence or human review.

## 3. Clothing and Textiles

Examples include shirts, trousers, coats, dresses, bags, and other textile products.

Relevant visible issues may include:

- Tears or holes
- Stains or discoloration
- Damaged seams
- Broken fasteners
- Visible fabric defects

Useful evidence may include a close-up of the defect and a wider photograph identifying the affected item.

The system must not assume that a stain or tear occurred before delivery solely from its appearance.

## 4. Footwear

Examples include shoes, boots, sandals, and sports footwear.

Relevant visible issues may include:

- Cracks or tears
- Damaged soles
- Stains
- Detached components
- Visible manufacturing defects

Useful evidence may include a close-up of the defect and a photograph identifying the affected shoe.

Signs of use may complicate the assessment. If the evidence does not establish whether damage was present at delivery, request human review.

## 5. Furniture and Home Products

Examples include tables, chairs, shelves, beds, decorative products, and household furnishings.

Relevant visible issues may include:

- Broken or cracked components
- Dents or scratches
- Damaged surfaces
- Deformation
- Missing visible parts

Useful evidence may include a photograph of the damage, a wider photograph of the item, and photographs of the packaging when relevant.

If assembly, installation, or handling may have contributed to the damage, the system must not assign responsibility without sufficient evidence.

## 6. Household Appliances

Examples include refrigerators, washing machines, ovens, vacuum cleaners, and small appliances.

Relevant visible issues may include:

- Dents
- Cracked housings
- Broken external components
- Damaged doors or handles
- Damaged packaging

A visual model cannot reliably establish all electrical, mechanical, or internal faults.

For functional failures, safety-related issues, or suspected internal damage, request human review and appropriate technical assessment.

## 7. Toys, Accessories, and Other Products

Examples include toys, watches, jewelry, sports equipment, books, and miscellaneous consumer goods.

The system should identify visible damage relevant to the specific item, such as cracks, tears, broken components, stains, or deformation.

If the product category is unknown or the relevant defect cannot be assessed using the available evidence, request clarification or human review.

## 8. Unknown or Unclassified Product Categories

The system must not refuse to assess a claim solely because the product category is not listed in this document.

It should apply the general policies and the relevant damage policy.

If a category-specific rule is necessary but unavailable, the system must state that the rule was not found and request human review.

## 9. Priority of Policies

Use the following interpretation:

1. Apply the general return policy where relevant.
2. Apply the general damage-on-delivery policy when damage is reported.
3. Consult category-specific guidance when it is relevant.
4. Consult evidence, escalation, and refund policies.
5. Refer unresolved or conflicting cases for human review.

Category-specific guidance must not override an explicit rule in an applicable approved policy without a defined precedence rule.

## 10. Disclaimer

This document contains fictional testing guidance. It does not establish the actual return conditions of any retailer or manufacturer.
